The Grand Strategy and Logistics of the Napoleonic Wars

The Italian Campaign and Revolutionary Doctrine

The military ascension of Napoleon Bonaparte cannot be decoupled from the foundational tactical evolutions of the late eighteenth century. Stepping into supreme command during the Italian Campaign of 1796, Bonaparte inherited a highly motivated but fundamentally disorganized French army. By enforcing strict adherence to rapid divisional mobility and internal lines of communication, he systematically isolated the separate contingents of the Piedmontese and Austrian coalitions.

Austerlitz and the Apogee of the Corps System

By December of 1805, the implementation of the independent Corps d'Armée system granted the Grand Armée an unmatched tactical fluidity. At the Battle of Austerlitz, frequently categorized as a masterpiece of tactical deception, Napoleon purposefully abandoned the strategic high ground of the Pratzen Heights. This calculated retreat enticed the combined Austro-Russian supreme command into launching an overextended assault on the French right flank.
